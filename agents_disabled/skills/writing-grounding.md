# Grounding

Every **concept** has to be **grounded** before a beat or block can lean on it: the reader either walked in knowing it or met it in an earlier beat or block. A beat or block that reaches for an ungrounded concept loses the reader. The unit is the concept, not the word for it: a beat or block can lean on an idea the reader lacks even with no jargon in sight. Where a concept has a name ( a **term** ) grounding it means landing the idea and the term together.

A concept gets grounded one of two ways:

- **Prerequisite** — grounded before the first beat or opening. The reader brings it. Fixed at the start.
- **Introduced** — a beat or block establishes it, and from then on it's grounded for every later beat or block.

So each beat or block does two jobs: it **requires** concepts that are already grounded, and it **grounds** new ones. Keep a running list of what's grounded so far, and update it each time a beat or block lands.

The lever is what you make a prerequisite versus what you ground inside the piece. Demand too much up front and you shut out readers who don't have it; ground too much inside and the early beats or blocks drown in definitions. Settle this with the user when you establish prerequisites, and revisit it whenever a tempting move turns out to require a concept nothing has grounded yet — the fix is either a grounding beat or block before it, or promoting the concept to a prerequisite.
